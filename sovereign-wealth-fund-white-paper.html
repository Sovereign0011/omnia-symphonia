What the President Asked For, and What Mai Had Already Started Building

Author: Johnathon Vasquez-Berg (Mai) Date: November 11, 2026 Version: 1.0

Abstract

On February 3, 2025, the President signed Executive Order 14196, "A Plan for Establishing a United States Sovereign Wealth Fund." It did not create a fund. It ordered a plan — Treasury and Commerce, within 90 days — for a fund that did not yet exist.

This paper sets that order side by side with what the author had already started building: the SAF architecture — the Sovereign Assistance Fund, three funds, an Automated Tithing Engine, transparent smart contracts, citizen-first access with no paywalls, and emotional-intelligence integration — submitted in his claim package of July 29, 2025, with a statement of authorship claiming the architecture was developed, simulated, and deployed prior to the Executive Order.

The paper presents both sides from their own documents, counts what the dated record does and does not show, and ends with the author's three requests: federal acknowledgment of contribution, review of the Sovereign System for optional integration, and advisory or licensing collaboration.

1. What the President Asked For — Executive Order 14196

Executive Order 14196, "A Plan for Establishing a United States Sovereign Wealth Fund," signed February 3, 2025. Published in the Federal Register February 10, 2025 (FR Doc. 2025-02477).

Section 1 — Policy and Purpose (as written)

"It is the policy of the United States to maximize the stewardship of our national wealth for the sole benefit of American citizens. To this end, it is in the interest of the American people that the Federal Government establish a sovereign wealth fund to promote fiscal sustainability, lessen the burden of taxes on American families and small businesses, establish economic security for future generations, and promote United States economic and strategic leadership internationally."

Section 2 — Sovereign Wealth Fund (as written)

"The Secretary of the Treasury and the Secretary of Commerce, in close coordination with the Assistant to the President for Economic Policy, shall develop a plan for the establishment of a sovereign wealth fund consistent with section 1 of this order. The Secretary of the Treasury and the Secretary of Commerce shall jointly submit this plan to the President within 90 days of the date of this order."

What the order did — and did not do

  • It did: order a plan, due within 90 days (by about May 4, 2025), covering structure, governance, funding mechanisms, investment strategy, and whether legislation was needed. It named no fund personnel and established no fund.
  • It did not: create a sovereign wealth fund, appropriate money, name a structure, or put a dollar in anything. An executive order alone cannot create one — that takes statute, or existing authority exercised deal by deal.

What happened after

  • May 2025: CBS reported the Bessent/Lutnick plan was submitted but the White House rejected parts of it; the White House said "no final decisions have yet been made."
  • May 16, 2025: Bloomberg, as relayed by Cato, reported the fund was put on the back burner after hitting legal, financial, and political limits.
  • May 23, 2025: the Treasury Secretary said the fund was "paused, not cancelled."
  • August 2025: the government took a 9.9% stake in Intel; the NEC Director called it "a down payment on a sovereign wealth fund."
  • As of October 2026: no formally chartered U.S. sovereign wealth fund entity exists, and no official White House, Treasury, or Commerce announcement says one has been created. Instead, an ad hoc portfolio of federal equity stakes (Intel, MP Materials, Lithium Americas, and others) exists across agencies, with Commerce and Treasury publicly split on structure. Treasury Secretary Bessent had said at the signing that the fund would be up and running within 12 months.

The 90-day plan itself was never made public. Not a page.

Until then, the claim stands as his claim, and the July 2025 documentation stands as the dated evidence.
